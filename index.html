Opportunities using current BCI technology

BCIs can encode and decode sensory information


  • Human-driven external processes and data generation

    Requires modeling human senses and actuators (SSN?)
    e.g. interoperable mechanical operations using human actions

  • Human-driven semantic data exploration

    Requires mapping link semantics to human actuators
    e.g. Web or Knowledge Graph navigation

BCI technology has major limitations

  • Limited understanding of neural encoding

    Abstract concepts and complex knowledge relations

  • Limited ability to integrate externally stored memories

    Prosthesis techniques exist for restoring or enhancing memory

  • Limited information bandwidth

    May be necessary to exchange large chunks of knowledge

BCIs are rapidly evolving due to increasing commercial interest

Mapping biological semantic memory to and from symbolic knowledge

  • Memories are combinations of personal multimodal experiences

    e.g. visual features, taste, smell, …

  • Concepts across people are not necessarily stored the same

    Personal experiences differ

  • Need for mappings to align biological memories across people

    Brain to RDF Mapping Language (B2RML)?

Mapping biological reasoning to and from (sub)symbolic reasoning

Augment human reasoning with external reasoners


  • (Sub)symbolic AI enables reasoning over digital knowledge

    → AI reasoning over brain knowledge
    → Human reasoning over external knowledge

  • Need for exchanging reasoning language and contexts

    Exchange LLM context windows, symbolic reasoning, proof languages, …

Modeling ownership and access control for biological memories

  • Memories are highly personal

    → Major ethical concerns

  • Need for strong access control and consent mechanisms

    Are current authorization mechanisms sufficient?
    E.g. RBAC, ACL, ODRL, …
